ST3189 - Linear Regression Quiz

1. In the context of linear regression, what does the term 'least squares' refer to?

1
The least squares method aims to find the line that minimizes the sum of the squared vertical distances (residuals) from each data point to the line. This is the most common method for fitting a linear regression model.
ISLP_website-1-pages-3.pdf, Section 3.1.1

2. What is the primary assumption about the relationship between predictors and the response in a simple linear regression model?

1
Simple linear regression assumes that there is an approximately linear relationship between the predictor X and the response Y. This can be written as \(Y \approx \beta_0 + \beta_1 X\).
ISLP_website-1-pages-3.pdf, Section 3.1

3. In the linear model \(Y = \beta_0 + \beta_1 X + \epsilon\), what does \(\beta_1\) represent?

2
\(\beta_1\) is the slope, which represents the average increase in the response Y for a one-unit increase in the predictor X.
ISLP_website-1-pages-3.pdf, Section 3.1.2

4. The Residual Sum of Squares (RSS) is defined as:

0
RSS is the sum of the squared differences between the observed response values \(y_i\) and the predicted response values \(\hat{y}_i\). It is defined as \(RSS = \sum_{i=1}^n (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2\).
ISLP_website-1-pages-3.pdf, Equation 3.3

5. What does an R-squared (\(R^2\)) value of 0.75 signify?

2
\(R^2\) is the proportion of variance in the response that is explained by the model. An \(R^2\) of 0.75 means that 75% of the variance in Y is explained by the predictors in the model.
ISLP_website-1-pages-3.pdf, Section 3.1.3

6. In multiple linear regression, what is the null hypothesis for the F-statistic?

2
The F-statistic tests the overall significance of the regression model. The null hypothesis (\(H_0\)) is that all regression coefficients are zero (\(\beta_1 = \beta_2 = ... = \beta_p = 0\)), meaning there is no relationship between the predictors and the response.
ISLP_website-1-pages-3.pdf, Section 3.2.2

7. What problem does multicollinearity refer to?

2
Collinearity (or multicollinearity) refers to the situation in which two or more predictor variables are closely related to one another. It can make it difficult to separate out the individual effects of collinear variables on the response.
ISLP_website-1-pages-3.pdf, Section 3.3.3

8. Which of the following is a potential solution for dealing with non-linearity in a linear regression model?

1
If the relationship between predictors and the response is non-linear, a simple approach is to use non-linear transformations of the predictors, such as \(\log(X)\), \(\sqrt{X}\), or \(X^2\), in the linear model.
ISLP_website-1-pages-3.pdf, Section 3.3.2

9. What is an interaction effect in a regression model?

0
An interaction effect, or synergy, occurs when the effect of one predictor variable on the response depends on the level of another predictor variable. It is included in the model by adding a term that is the product of the two interacting predictors.
ISLP_website-1-pages-3.pdf, Section 3.3.2

10. What is the purpose of a prediction interval?

1
A prediction interval provides a range where a single future observation is likely to fall, given certain values of the predictors. It accounts for both the uncertainty in the estimated regression line (reducible error) and the random error term \(\epsilon\) (irreducible error).
ISLP_website-1-pages-3.pdf, Section 3.2.2

11. In the context of linear regression, what is a 'loss function'?

2
A loss function measures the 'cost' of the model's predictions being wrong. In linear regression, the most common loss function is the squared error loss, \((y_i - f(x_i))^2\), which is minimized to find the best model parameters.
fciml-pages-1.pdf, Section 1.1.3

12. What is the primary goal of the 'least squares' approach in linear modelling?

1
The least squares approach seeks to find the optimal values for the model parameters by minimizing the average loss function, which is typically the mean of the squared differences between the observed target values and the values predicted by the model.
fciml-pages-1.pdf, Section 1.1.4

13. In vector/matrix notation for linear regression, \(y = Xw + \epsilon\), what does the matrix \(X\) represent?

2
\(X\) is the design matrix. Each row corresponds to an observation, and each column corresponds to a feature (or basis function of a feature). It is often augmented with a column of ones to account for the intercept term.
fciml-pages-1.pdf, Section 1.3

14. What is the primary difference between a parametric and a non-parametric statistical learning approach?

1
Parametric methods reduce the problem of estimating f down to one of estimating a set of parameters because they assume a specific functional form (e.g., a linear model). Non-parametric methods do not make explicit assumptions about the form of f and seek to estimate it by getting as close to the data points as possible.
ISLP_website-1-pages-2.pdf, Section 2.1.3

15. What is the main disadvantage of a very flexible (complex) statistical learning model?

2
More flexible models have a higher risk of overfitting. They can fit the training data very well, but they may be capturing the random noise rather than the true underlying signal. This leads to poor performance on new, unseen data.
ISLP_website-1-pages-2.pdf, Section 2.1.3

16. The mean squared error (MSE) is composed of which two quantities?

1
The expected test MSE can be decomposed into the sum of three fundamental quantities: the variance of the model's predictions, the squared bias of the model, and the variance of the irreducible error term \(\epsilon\). This is known as the bias-variance decomposition.
ISLP_website-1-pages-2.pdf, Section 2.2.2

17. What is the 'bias-variance trade-off'?

1
The bias-variance trade-off is a key concept in machine learning. Simple, less flexible models tend to have high bias and low variance. Complex, more flexible models tend to have low bias and high variance. The goal is to find a model that has low bias and low variance, minimizing the total error.
ISLP_website-1-pages-2.pdf, Section 2.2.2

18. In the context of linear regression, what is the purpose of the 't-statistic'?

2
The t-statistic is used to assess the significance of an individual predictor. It measures how many standard errors a coefficient is away from zero. A large absolute t-statistic (and a corresponding small p-value) provides evidence against the null hypothesis that the coefficient is zero.
ISLP_website-1-pages-3.pdf, Section 3.1.2

19. What is a 'dummy variable' used for in linear regression?

1
Dummy variables are used to include qualitative predictors in a regression model. For a predictor with two levels, a single dummy variable is created that takes on a value of 0 or 1. For a predictor with more than two levels, multiple dummy variables are created.
ISLP_website-1-pages-3.pdf, Section 3.3.1

20. What does the 'Residual Standard Error' (RSE) measure?

0
The RSE is an estimate of the standard deviation of the error term \(\epsilon\). Roughly speaking, it is the average amount that the response will deviate from the true regression line.
ISLP_website-1-pages-3.pdf, Section 3.1.3

21. What is the primary purpose of using subset selection methods like best subset selection or forward stepwise selection?

1
Subset selection methods are used to choose a smaller set of predictors from a larger pool. This can improve model interpretability by removing irrelevant variables and can also improve prediction accuracy by reducing model variance.
Subset Selection Linear Regression(2).html

22. Which of the following is a drawback of best subset selection?

1
Best subset selection requires fitting \(2^p\) models, which is computationally prohibitive for even moderate values of p (e.g., p > 40).
Subset Selection Linear Regression(2).html

23. How does forward stepwise selection work?

2
Forward stepwise selection is a greedy algorithm that begins with a null model (intercept only) and iteratively adds the single predictor that results in the largest decrease in RSS, until a stopping rule is satisfied.
Subset Selection Linear Regression(2).html

24. What is the main idea behind shrinkage methods like Ridge and Lasso regression?

1
Shrinkage methods fit a model containing all p predictors but constrain or regularize the coefficient estimates, shrinking them towards zero. This can reduce the variance of the model and improve prediction accuracy.
Shrinkage Methods(3).html

25. What is the key difference between the penalty used in Ridge regression and Lasso regression?

2
Ridge regression uses an L2 penalty, which is the sum of the squared coefficients. Lasso regression uses an L1 penalty, which is the sum of the absolute values of the coefficients. This difference in penalty leads to different behaviors in coefficient shrinkage.
Shrinkage Methods(3).html

26. A key advantage of Lasso regression over Ridge regression is that:

1
The L1 penalty in Lasso has the effect of forcing some coefficient estimates to be exactly zero when the tuning parameter \(\lambda\) is sufficiently large. This allows Lasso to perform automatic variable selection, resulting in a sparse model.
Shrinkage Methods(3).html

27. In Bayesian linear regression, what is the role of the prior distribution?

2
The prior distribution, \(\pi(\theta)\), is a key component of Bayesian inference. It is assigned to the unknown parameters to represent our beliefs or uncertainty about them before any data is observed.
Bayesian Linear Regression.html

28. The posterior distribution in Bayesian inference is proportional to:

2
According to Bayes' theorem, the posterior distribution is proportional to the product of the likelihood function and the prior distribution: \(\pi(\theta|y) \propto f(y|\theta)\pi(\theta)\).
Bayesian Inference Essentials Part 1(1).html

29. Ridge regression can be viewed as a Bayesian linear regression model with what kind of prior on the coefficients?

2
Ridge regression is equivalent to finding the posterior mode (MAP estimate) when the regression coefficients \(\beta\) are given a Gaussian prior distribution with a mean of zero.
Bayesian Linear Regression.html

30. Lasso regression is equivalent to finding the MAP estimate under which prior distribution for the coefficients?

1
The Lasso estimate can be interpreted as a Bayesian posterior mode estimate under independent Laplace priors for the coefficients. The shape of the Laplace prior encourages sparsity.
Bayesian Linear Regression.html

31. What is a 'confidence interval' in frequentist inference?

2
The frequentist interpretation of a 95% confidence interval is that if we were to generate many datasets and construct an interval for each, 95% of these intervals would contain the true, unknown parameter value.
Statistical Inference on Linear Regression Models.html

32. What is a 'credible interval' in Bayesian inference?

1
A Bayesian credible interval has a more direct probabilistic interpretation. A 95% credible interval is a range for which we believe there is a 95% probability that it contains the true parameter value, based on the posterior distribution.
Bayesian Inference Essentials Part 1(1).html

33. What is the primary purpose of using basis functions (e.g., polynomials, splines) in a linear model?

2
Basis functions are transformations of the original predictors. By using functions like \(X^2, X^3, \log(X)\), or splines as predictors, we can fit non-linear relationships within the framework of a linear model (i.e., the model is still linear in the coefficients).
The Linear Regression Model.html

34. What is a 'spline' in the context of regression?

2
Splines are a flexible way to model non-linearities. They involve fitting separate low-degree polynomials over different regions of the predictor space. These polynomials are joined smoothly at points called knots.
Spline-Based Methods.html

35. What is a key advantage of using a natural cubic spline over a regular cubic spline?

2
Polynomials can have high variance at the boundaries. Natural splines add extra constraints, forcing the function to be linear beyond the boundary knots. This often improves the stability of the fit near the edges of the predictor's range.
Spline-Based Methods.html

36. What is the main idea behind Generalized Additive Models (GAMs)?

1
GAMs extend the linear model by allowing non-linear functions for each predictor, while the model remains additive. The model takes the form \(y_i = \beta_0 + f_1(x_{i1}) + f_2(x_{i2}) + ... + f_p(x_{ip}) + \epsilon_i\), where each \(f_j\) can be a smooth non-linear function (like a spline).
Spline-Based Methods.html

37. What is a potential problem with fitting a linear regression model when the error terms \(\epsilon_i\) are correlated?

2
The standard formulas for computing the standard errors of the regression coefficients assume that the error terms are uncorrelated. If this assumption is violated, the standard errors will be underestimated, causing confidence intervals and p-values to be narrower and smaller than they should be.
ISLP_website-1-pages-3.pdf, Section 3.3.3

38. What is an 'outlier' in a regression context?

1
An outlier is an observation for which the response \(y_i\) is unusual given the predictor \(x_i\). Outliers can have a significant impact on the least squares fit, although their effect is often less than that of high leverage points.
ISLP_website-1-pages-3.pdf, Section 3.3.3

39. What is a 'high leverage' point?

2
High leverage points are observations with unusual values for the predictor variables \(x_i\). These points have the potential to exert a strong influence on the fitted regression line, pulling it towards themselves.
ISLP_website-1-pages-3.pdf, Section 3.3.3

40. The Gauss-Markov theorem states that under certain assumptions, the least squares estimator is:

2
The Gauss-Markov theorem provides a theoretical justification for the use of least squares. It states that if the linear regression model assumptions hold (linearity, homoscedasticity, no autocorrelation), the OLS estimator is the most efficient (i.e., has the lowest variance) within the class of all linear unbiased estimators.
Statistical Inference on Linear Regression Models.html

41. What does 'homoscedasticity' mean in the context of linear regression?

1
Homoscedasticity is the assumption that the error terms \(\epsilon_i\) have a constant variance, \(Var(\epsilon_i) = \sigma^2\). The opposite, where the variance changes with the level of the predictors, is called heteroscedasticity.
ISLP_website-1-pages-3.pdf, Section 3.3.3

42. What is the primary purpose of examining residual plots in regression diagnostics?

2
Residual plots are a crucial diagnostic tool. By plotting the residuals against the fitted values or the predictors, we can identify patterns that suggest a violation of the model assumptions, such as a non-linear trend (indicating the linear model is inadequate) or a funnel shape (indicating heteroscedasticity).
ISLP_website-1-pages-3.pdf, Section 3.3.3

43. If you fit a multiple linear regression model and the p-value for the F-statistic is very small, but the p-values for individual coefficients are large, what is a likely cause?

1
This is a classic symptom of multicollinearity. The F-test shows that the predictors as a group are significant, but because they are highly correlated, the model cannot disentangle their individual effects, leading to high standard errors and insignificant p-values for each one.
ISLP_website-1-pages-3.pdf, Section 3.3.3

44. What is the 'curse of dimensionality'?

1
The curse of dimensionality refers to the fact that as the number of features (p) grows, the data becomes increasingly sparse in the p-dimensional space. This means that the distance to the nearest neighbors tends to become very large, making local methods like k-NN less effective.
ISLP_website-1-pages-3.pdf, Section 3.5

45. In the formula for the least squares estimators \(\hat{\beta} = (X^T X)^{-1}X^T y\), what must be true about the matrix \(X^T X\)?

1
For the least squares solution to exist and be unique, the matrix \(X^T X\) must be invertible (non-singular). This is not the case if there is perfect multicollinearity among the predictors, as one column of X would be a linear combination of the others.
Statistical Inference on Linear Regression Models.html

46. Which of these is NOT a standard assumption of the classical linear regression model?

3
The classical linear regression model does not assume that the predictors themselves are normally distributed. The key assumptions relate to the error term: they should have a mean of zero, constant variance, be normally distributed, and be uncorrelated with each other.
The Linear Regression Model.html

47. What is the primary difference between a confidence interval and a prediction interval for a given value of X?

1
A confidence interval is for the average response \(E(Y|X=x)\). A prediction interval is for a single future observation \(Y\). The prediction interval is always wider because it must account for both the uncertainty in the model's fit and the irreducible error of the individual data point.
ISLP_website-1-pages-3.pdf, Section 3.2.2

48. What does the 'Adjusted R-squared' do that the regular R-squared does not?

1
The standard \(R^2\) will always increase when more variables are added to the model, even if they are not truly associated with the response. The adjusted \(R^2\) penalizes the addition of useless variables and will only increase if the new variable improves the model more than would be expected by chance.
Subset Selection Linear Regression(2).html

49. Which method is considered a 'greedy' approach to subset selection?

2
Forward and backward stepwise selection are considered greedy algorithms because they make the best choice at each step without looking ahead to see if a different choice might lead to a better overall model in the future. This makes them computationally efficient but not guaranteed to find the absolute best model.
Subset Selection Linear Regression(2).html

50. The elastic net penalty is a combination of which two other penalties?

1
The elastic net is a compromise between ridge and lasso regression. Its penalty term is a mix of the L1 (Lasso) and L2 (Ridge) penalties, controlled by a mixing parameter. It can select variables like lasso while handling correlated predictors more effectively like ridge.
Shrinkage Methods(3).html
